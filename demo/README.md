## Script

1. This is Line Launch Desk, an independent concept I built for ClaimSorted's Founding Ops and Strategy role. Every client, rule and claim here is synthetic.

2. Start with the portfolio. The six lines on ClaimSorted's site each get a fictional client, run through one gate. Warranty is ready. General liability holds: old wording, a slow payment SLA, and no complaint or reporting rule. All illustrative.

3. New wording often arrives as a document, not a spreadsheet. I pick the travel sample and draft. A rule based extractor, with no API, quotes the source sentence for every row. Hedged clauses like reasonable costs are flagged for a human, never invented.

4. Send the draft to import. Nothing is rejected, but every drafted row has no owner and no client sign-off, so the gate holds.

5. A fictional pet insurer sends the handling spec for a new line. Fourteen rows. I validate it against one shared claims framework.

6. Three rows are rejected outright. One identical duplicate is dropped by its rule ID, never by its name. And the eight stage framework shows what's missing: no complaint route, and no usable reporting rule.

7. So the gate says hold. Handler authority is above the cap, the payment SLA is too slow, and a coverage rule cites last year's policy wording. Publishing is refused.

8. The client's revised spec covers all eight stages, so the gate turns ready. Both sign-offs are tied to this exact pack hash.

9. Publish writes once. A second publish is a no-op, and a fresh read verifies the hash. It's a simulated write, in this browser only.

10. In the pilot, the blended median cycle time falls from ten days to four. But simple and complex claims both got slower. That's case mix, so the desk says no decision.

11. Handler three used the new playbook step on twelve of twenty seven claims. That goes on the product agenda as something to test, not a finding.

12. Gaps caught before go live, sign-offs tied to exact versions, and metrics that show their denominators. That's Line Launch Desk.
